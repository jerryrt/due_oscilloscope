### SAM3X differential mode (quoted, `docs/datasheets/microchip/SAM3X-SAM3A-11057C.pdf`)

- Pair table, Table 43-5 (Section 43.6.8, printed p. 1327): `AD6-AD7 -> CH6`. With the Arduino labels `A1 = AD6`, `A0 = AD7` (CLAUDE.md), CH1 = A1 and CH2 = A0 are exactly one differential pair, and the converter forms (AD6 - AD7) = (A1 - A0) = (CH1 - CH2) if the first-listed pin is VIN+. The datasheet draws VIN+ and VIN- in Figure 43-6 (p. 1328) but the pair table does not say which pin is which *(check; closed by VT-11, the sign test)*. DF-1's 'CH1 = IN+' needs exactly that mapping.
- Gain, Table 43-6 (p. 1327) and ADC_CGR (p. 1349): with DIFF = 1, GAIN code 00 -> 0.5, 01 -> 1, 10 -> 2, 11 -> 2. DF-2's two ranges are code 01 (PGA 1, +/-5 V difference) and code 00 (PGA 0.5, +/-10 V difference). Offset (Table 43-7) is not available in differential mode.
- Scale, Section 45.7.1 (p. 1403-1404): differential LSB = AREF/4096 at gain 1 and 2 x AREF/4096 at gain 0.5 (the datasheet's example: 6.0 V/4096 = 1465 uV at ADVREF 3.0 V). Full scale is therefore +/-AREF/2 (PGA 1) and +/-AREF (PGA 0.5) of **pin difference**.
- Pin range, Table 45-38 (Section 45.7.2, printed p. ~1407 *(check page)*): VIR 0 to VADVREF; leakage 0.5 uA; Ci 8 pF; 'up to VDDIN without destruction'. **The datasheet text read here states no separate common-mode window for differential mode** - only Figure 43-6, which can be read as each pin staying inside 0..AREF. DF-2's '*(check)*' stands: this report holds every pin inside 0..AREF with at least 80 mV of margin (the SC-2 criterion) and leaves the converter's own common-mode behaviour to VT-2.
- Static errors in differential mode, Tables 45-32/45-33 (p. 1404-1405): INL +/-1 LSB typ, +/-1.4 LSB max (VDDIN 3.0-3.6 V); offset error -22 / +11.5 / +48 LSB; gain error -1.56 / -0.56 / +0.29 %. These are the Due's, not the AFE's, and bear on DF-4 (below).

## What this step found, before the tables

*This section and the three after it were written against the previous DF-3 wording (46 dB at DC and 40 dB at 10 kHz before any calibration, closed by 0.05 % / 10 ppm/K parts); the amended limits (30 dB / 18 dB before calibration, 46 dB after the PA-3 calibration) and the stocked-part variants are judged in the section above. The `spec` and `fixed` figures below are unchanged in method.*

1. **DF-3 is not met by the design as specified (0.1 % parts), at its worst corners, and the cause is not the resistors.** Worst signed corner, DC: **32.0 dB on the +/-5 V range** (limit 46) and **41.2 dB on the +/-1.25 V range**; at 10 kHz **20.2 dB / 21.1 dB** (limit 40). Even the Monte Carlo (no corner stacking) has a minimum of 34.8 dB at DC on the +/-5 V range and 23.5 dB at 10 kHz, and a **median of 43.5 dB against the 'typical 50 dB'** on that range.
   - +/-5 V range, DC: the range switch's **Ron sits in the gain** (G = 1 + Ron/RG, RG = 249 ohm). Ron is 3.7 ohm typ / 8.8 ohm max with no minimum or unit-to-unit spread given, so two channels can differ by up to 5.1 ohm = 2.0 % of gain = 34 dB of CMRR. This is the 2b 'Ron spread' cost showing up as the largest term. 0.1 % resistors alone would give 41 dB; the 0.1 % does not close the budget even without Ron.
   - +/-1.25 V range, DC: every resistor at its 0.1 % limit plus the 25 ppm/K tempco over 10 K gives 0.87 % gain mismatch (divider 0.38 %, RF/RG 0.40 %, op-amp CMRR 0.08 %, source resistance 0.01 %): 41.2 dB. The budget script's 50 dB (`budget/afe_budget.py`, which counted R1/R2 only on the superseded 237k divider) was optimistic by the R3, RF, RG, tempco and op-amp terms.
   - 10 kHz: the compensated divider's C1/C2 tolerance. A 5 % error in C1 or C2 mis-compensates one channel's divider by up to about 9 % of its HF gain, and at 10 kHz (the divider corner is 17 kHz) half of that is visible: **20 dB at the worst corner**, far from 40 dB. No 0.1 % resistor choice touches it; no fixed C0G part is tight enough (needs about 0.5 % capacitors *and* 0.1 pF stray matching, below), so **C1 has to be trimmed per channel on the bench**.
2. **What closes it (rerun as scenario `fixed`, all numbers below):** (a) resistors 0.05 % with a temperature coefficient of at most 10 ppm/K (thin film or a matched network, ratio tracking) *(check availability and cost against the low-cost rule)*; (b) the range switches of the two channels matched in Ron to within 0.5 ohm of each other (one dual package or selected pairs; the TMUX1119 datasheet gives no matching figure *(check)*); (c) C1 trimmed on the bench, per channel, to within 0.5 % of the value that flattens that channel's own response (about 0.06 pF of 12 pF, VT-2's 10 kHz CMRR is the trim criterion). Result: DC worst corner **45.4 dB (+/-5 V) and 46.5 dB (+/-1.25 V)**, 10 kHz worst corner **41.9 / 42.6 dB**, Monte Carlo minima 51.6 / 52.9 dB at DC and 46.5 / 48.2 dB at 10 kHz. The DC margin at the corner is thin (-0.55 dB); the sweep tables below say which of the three levers buys what.
3. **DF-2 holds** on both ranges; the one number worth the owner's attention is the common-mode limit: each input individually must stay within about **+/-6.20 V on the +/-5 V range** and **+/-1.30 V on the +/-1.25 V range**, so a +/-10 V difference leaves a common mode of only about +/-1.2 V, and +/-2.5 V on the 1.25 V range leaves about +/-0.05 V.
4. **DF-4 is limited by something the per-channel gain/offset fit cannot remove**: the channel's own non-linearity, which in the vendor op-amp model is the input-stage crossover, and (outside the AFE) the converter's INL of up to 1.4 LSB. See DF-4.

| +/-1.25 V | 1.1153 - 1.1175 | +/-1.432 V | +/-2.86 V (converter); pins limit it to about +/-2.67 V with 80 mV margin | 0.722 mV | 1.445 mV |

**Largest common-mode voltage (both inputs at +V) for which each pin stays in range:** 6.25 V on the +/-5 V range (top limit, AREF 3.20 V, centring +30 mV, rail 3.23 V; 6.50 V at AREF 3.30) and 1.30 V on the +/-1.25 V range. The negative side is the mirror: on the +/-5 V range the pin never gets within 80 mV of 0 V in this model because the op-amp output stops at about 0.1 V first (clipping at -6.20 V); on the +/-1.25 V range the 80 mV margin is lost at -1.30 V. Since the channels are independent, **the window is a per-input rule**: with a difference Vd, the common mode may be at most about (limit - |Vd|/2), i.e. 3.7 V for the +/-5 V difference and 1.2 V for +/-10 V on the +/-5 V range, 0.68 V for +/-1.25 V and 0.05 V for +/-2.5 V on the +/-1.25 V range.

**Past the limit** the op-amp output stops at (rail - its output swing) at the top and (about 0.1 V) at the bottom; the ADC pin therefore never leaves 0..AREF in this model (top end 3.12 V at rail 3.23 V), no clamp current flows, and nothing is damaged (Table 45-38: up to VDDIN without destruction; PR-1/PR-2 are 2b's). What is lost is accuracy: each channel reads a clipped value and the difference is wrong by the mismatch of the two clips. In the sim both channels are the same model and clip at nearly the same level, so the peak pin-difference error over the sweep is the last column of the table above (50-53 mV on the +/-5 V range, about 94 mV on the +/-1.25 V range; it is dominated by what is not clipping - the finite CMRR of the spec corner times the common mode, and on the 1.25 V range the pair's DC offset of up to 85 mV - not by the clip mismatch, which the identical models make zero); **with real parts the two outputs' swing differs by an amount the datasheet bounds only at 5 V *(check)*.** The converter's own behaviour at the edge of its differential common-mode window is not in the datasheet text read here and is **not simulated** (VT-2 sweeps common mode at fixed difference on the actual Due).

The datasheet gives the OPA354's output swing from the rail as 0.1 V typ / 0.3 V max at a 5 V supply only (`datasheet-facts.md`). If it were 0.3 V at 3.23 V the top clip would sit at 2.93 V, i.e. the top common-mode limit would fall from about 6.2 V to about 5.3 V and a +/-6 V input would clip on the +/-5 V range. That is 2b's open item (VT-1), and it bounds this one.

## DF-4: accuracy after calibration

Method: for every draw each channel is fitted with a gain and an offset at the reference temperature (25 C), other input grounded, nine points over the range, by least squares (that is what a per-channel two-parameter host calibration can remove). The same 81 input pairs are then read at the draw's temperature (+/-10 K, resistors with an independent tempco up to the stated value, offset drift up to 4 uV/K) and the input-referred error of the difference is compared with the allowance 0.5 % of the reading + one code (one code = LSB / difference gain at AREF 3.30 V, PGA 1 or 0.5). The 1024-sample mean removes the converter's noise (the difference's noise below is 0.26 code) and quantisation; it removes neither of the two terms here.

| scenario / range | error at the input, worst (mV) | of which channel non-linearity at 25 C (mV) | drift 15-35 C only (mV) | one code (PGA 1 / PGA 0.5) | worst error / allowance (PGA 1 / PGA 0.5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| spec, +/-5 V | 12.23 | 5.96 | 9.53 | 3.43 / 6.86 mV | 2.81 / 1.41 |
| spec, +/-1.25 V | 4.37 | 1.97 | 3.92 | 0.72 / 1.45 mV | 5.49 / 2.75 |
| fixed, +/-5 V | 9.82 | 5.93 | 6.79 | 3.43 / 6.86 mV | 2.02 / 1.01 |
| fixed, +/-1.25 V | 2.96 | 1.97 | 1.78 | 0.72 / 1.44 mV | 2.53 / 1.26 |

**What remains after the per-channel fit** is therefore (1) the **drift** of the two channels' gain and offset between the calibration temperature and the operating temperature - the column above - made of the resistors' tempco (at most 25 ppm/K independent -> 0.038 % of gain per channel per 10 K for the divider alone; the sum is in the table) and the op-amp offset drift (4 uV/K x 10 K = 40 uV x the stage gain 1 or 4.83 at the pin); the shared Vmid and sense-node drift is common to both pins and cancels to the CMRR; (2) the channel's own **non-linearity**, which a gain and offset cannot remove: in the vendor model it is the OPA354 input-stage crossover near V+ - 2 V: 5.9 mV referred to the input on the +/-5 V range (1.7 codes at PGA 1) and 2.0 mV on the +/-1.25 V range (2.7 codes, 0.71 mV each); the +/-5 V range passes only because 0.5 % of a large reading is allowed as well; (3) the common-mode-proportional term: after the fit the error at zero difference but a common mode of +/-6 V (+/-1.25 V) is up to 6.8 mV (1.8 mV on the 1.25 V range). It is not 'a percentage of reading' - a reading near zero in a large common mode carries it fully - and DF-4 as worded cannot bound it as a percent.

**What this means for DF-4:** on the +/-5 V range the fixed design leaves the AFE's contribution inside the allowance; on the +/-1.25 V range it does not at PGA 1, because the crossover non-linearity (one model sample, *(check)*, VT-1) is several of that range's 0.71 mV codes. The datasheet bounds the OPA354 offset at +/-8 mV over the whole input range without giving the crossover step, so the real part could be better or worse than this model. **Requirement problem for the owner:** the converter's own INL is +/-1 LSB typ and +/-1.4 LSB max in differential mode (Table 45-32), so ' +/-1 code ' is already used up by the converter before the AFE adds anything, and a gain/offset calibration cannot remove it. DF-4 can be met only with a piecewise (multi-point) host correction of each channel, or by reading ' code ' as the converter's own and counting the AFE separately. The OPA365 (0.2 mV Vos, no crossover) would remove the AFE part but fails 2b's SM-3 bandwidth on the +/-1.25 V range (7.1 MHz against 8).

## Problems with the requirements, for the owner

1. **DF-3 'parts of 0.1 % tolerance or better' is not sufficient** and is silent on what dominates: the range switch's Ron match (+/-5 V range), the resistors' tempco, the op-amp CMRR and, at 10 kHz, the capacitors. Adding 'switch Ron matched within 0.5 ohm, resistors 0.05 % with at most 10 ppm/K, C1 trimmed per channel' would make the requirement testable; none of the three is a 0.1 % statement.
2. **'Typical 50 dB at DC'** is 43.8 dB median on the +/-5 V range as specified (the Ron spread), against 61 dB on the +/-1.25 V range.
3. **DF-4 '+/-0.5 % of reading +/-1 code'** cannot be met by any analog front end when the converter's own INL is up to 1.4 LSB, cannot bound a common-mode-proportional error as a percentage of a small reading, and needs a multi-point calibration for the channel's non-linearity.
4. **DF-2 '+/-10 V difference with each input within +/-6 V'** implies a common mode of at most about +/-1 V at the extremes, and the +/-1.25 V range's '+/-2.5 V' has none. Worth saying in the user-facing text.
5. `budget/afe_budget.py` DF-3 (50 dB) used the superseded 237k divider and only R1/R2; it should be superseded by this report's figures (not edited here).
