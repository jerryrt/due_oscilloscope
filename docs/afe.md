# AFE requirements

A minimal analog front end (AFE) for the Due scope and AWG: protection, level
scaling and a bipolar generator output in front of the Due's native 12-bit ADC
and DAC, with two capture modes (real-time and equivalent-time). Two scope
channels, one AWG output, and a differential mode selected in host software (Phase 1),
and a precision calibration channel (Phase 3), both in version 1. The plan that builds it is
[afe-plan.md](afe-plan.md).

The AFE is designed, simulated, built and verified against the numbers below.

## 3. Scope and phasing

The AFE adds protection, level scaling and a bipolar generator output to the
Due's native converters. The design topology, part values and part choices are
the contractor's. Everything here is a requirement, a constraint, a deliverable
or an acceptance test.

| Phase | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Two single-ended scope channels, one AWG output, differential mode selected in host software |
| 3 | A precision reference and a slow precision ADC (section 9, D5 adopted) |

Both phases are **one PCB design**, built over several rounds. **Version 1 carries Phase 1, 3a and 3b.** What a round does not populate is an unfitted footprint (MC-11), so a later phase is not a new board.
## 4. The Due as measured

The Due is the fixed part of this design, and it has been characterised to its
limits. This section is its baseline: facts the requirements below rest on,
each with its source. A figure with no record behind it says so. The three Dues
differ, and every per-board value is the client's to supply.

### 4.1 The board

## 5. Capture modes

The scope is a general-purpose instrument with two capture modes on one wide,
unfiltered path. Real-time capture takes any signal at the Due's sample rate.
Equivalent-time capture folds a repeating signal of known content to a much finer
time axis, which is what channel estimation and signal-integrity checks need:
eye diagrams, edges and level separation of NRZ and PAM test patterns, among
other repeating signals (SM-7 says which have a host path today). Equivalent-time
capture is measured on this hardware (section 4.2): the host half works, and what
remains is an AFE that lets it see a signal other than the DAC's.

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| SM-3 | **One wide path for both modes.** Equivalent-time (a repeating signal of known content): the host folds the samples by symbol phase. Real-time: single shot, any signal, at the Due's sample rate. The AFE has no anti-alias filter, and the bandwidth is at least 8 MHz on the ±5 V range and at least 7 MHz on the ±1.25 V range (D3, 2026-11-11), confirmed by simulation at G1. **The 8 MHz is a floor, not a target, and nothing in the AFE needs to work above 10 MHz (D13)**; **it is read at the Due's ADC pin (D20)**, since the Due's own input pole (about 7 MHz at its sampling capacitor, derived) is the Due's limit and no AFE design can move it: with no filter (SC-5) the ADC folds in every frequency the stage passes, so bandwidth beyond the need adds noise against SC-6 and buys nothing. Real-time capture aliases anything above the Nyquist frequency, which the host cannot detect or remove, and the documentation and the host's display say so. The AFE's noise over this band is bounded by SC-6 |
| SM-6 | In real-time capture, a differential signal is measured with the ADC's differential mode (DF-2), not by subtracting two single-ended channels (its common-mode rejection is DF-3's before-calibration figure): the channels convert about 0.95 µs apart, so subtraction pairs samples taken at different times, which on an edge reads as a false difference. In equivalent-time capture each channel is folded on its own time axis, so the skew can in principle be corrected on the host; this is not demonstrated |
| SM-7 | **Host, client-supplied and measured:** the fold's time axis is 0.24 ns. The tracker locks with a template of a known repeating pattern, so a repeating signal of unknown content has no demonstrated host path, and single-shot events are out of reach by construction. The ADC and DAC rate pair follows the rule of [ets-mode.md](ets-mode.md) (an odd DAC rate constant with an even ADC one on two boards, coprime on one) or the lock degrades |
| SM-8 | The wide path can be exercised by the Due's own DAC through the unfiltered AWG output and an external cable (AW-3, MC-10), so it is verifiable without an external source |

**How many PAM levels a signal of a given amplitude can carry** on the ±5 V
range, derived from the same-board, probes-free level band of 8.6 to 11.4 codes
(section 4.2) at the SC-4 step of 3.5 to 4 mV per code: 30 to 46 mV at the input.
The eye closes when the spacing, amplitude over N−1, falls below the band. Not
measured through an AFE. The ±1.25 V range (SC-11) has about 4.6 to 4.8 times the ±5 V
range's gain, so every count rises by that factor for a signal that fits it.

| Signal, peak to peak | Levels that fit | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 V | 12 to 17 | PAM8 passes (71 mV spacing), PAM16 (33 mV) is on the limit |
| 1 V | 23 to 34 | PAM16 passes (67 mV), PAM32 (32 mV) is on the limit |
| 2 V | 45 to 67 | PAM32 passes (65 mV), PAM64 (32 mV) is on the limit |
| 5 V | 111 to 167 | PAM64 passes (79 mV), PAM128 (39 mV) is on the limit |
| 10 V, the full ±5 V | 220 to 333 | PAM128 passes (79 mV), PAM256 (39 mV) is on the limit |

With a single ±5 V range the AFE reaches PAM16 on the 1 V signals a PAM scope is
usually pointed at. SC-11 is what changes that.

## 6. Scope input (CH1 to A1, CH2 to A0)


| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| SC-1 | Default: two independent single-ended DC-coupled channels |
| SC-2 | Each input ±5 V, usable to ±6 V with the ADC pin inside 0 to AREF, with at least 80 mV of margin per side at AREF 3.20 V. This holds over AREF 3.20 to 3.30 V, component tolerance and a midpoint centring error of ±30 mV. The AREF spread is shown by analysis (VT-13) and by measuring each board as found, not by changing AREF |
| SC-3 | **Input resistance 1 MΩ per input, through a compensated divider, so the inputs take ×1 and ×10 probes (2026-11-11: restored when SM-5 was dropped; the 20 to 30 kΩ input existed only for SM-5).** Input capacitance at most 30 pF including wiring |
| SC-4 | At most 4 mV per ADC code at the input. ±5 V occupies 70 to 75% of ADC full scale at nominal AREF, and up to about 76% at AREF 3.20 V. At 70% the step is 3.5 mV, and the 75% end leaves about 10% of full scale at ±6 V |
| SC-5 | **No filter (decided 2026-11-10).** The scope path is the one wide path of SM-3, with no low-pass beyond the bandwidth the stages have. Gain error across the band is corrected by host calibration. No band limit is added to meet noise (2026-11-11): SC-6 and AW-6 are met by the parts' own noise at the stages' bandwidth. The AFE is not an anti-alias filter, and its documentation must say so |
| SC-6 | AFE noise referred to the ADC pin at most **0.2 mV rms (decided 2026-11-10)** over the whole bandwidth of the one wide path (SM-3), on both ranges, stated as a density at the pin (0.2 mV over 10 MHz is 63 nV per root hertz, derived). The ADC's sample-and-hold folds every frequency the pin carries into the sample, so a figure over 1 MHz does not bound a wide path. Against the Due's floor of 0.8 to 0.85 codes (0.64 to 0.68 mV, section 4.1) the chosen 0.2 mV widens it by 4 to 5%; for comparison 0.5 mV would widen it by 24 to 27%, 0.3 mV by 9 to 10% and 0.1 mV by about 1%. An AFE noise below about a fifth of the floor it adds to (2% growth) is over-design. The Due's ADC cannot verify this directly: it is shown by simulation (AN-5) and by an external instrument where one is available (VT-3, VT-16), and its effect is measured through VT-15. **What is integrated (2026-11-12, D17):** the noise that reaches the Due's sampling capacitor, through the stage and the ADC input's own network (the isolation resistor, the pin, the input switch at its worst-case resistance, and the sampling capacitor), with no cut-off at 10 MHz: D13 bounds the signal, not the noise, and noise above it still folds into every sample |
| SC-7 | DC accuracy after host calibration: ±0.5% of reading ±5 mV, for the mean of at least 1024 samples. **How the stack is judged (2026-11-12, D26):** terms with a common cause are added linearly; independent terms, each taken at its worst-case bound, are combined root-sum-square, and the stack passes when that total is inside ±0.5% and ±5 mV. The plain worst-case sum is reported beside it as information. **The calibration fit's share of it, the tee's fit and its AWG sense together, is 0.1% on both ranges (2026-11-11)**: on ±5 V the tee's AWG signal covers about 23% of the range (PA-3), and on ±1.25 V the sense reading's bound alone is about 0.05%; **with the E-grade reference (PA-2) and no meter calibration (2026-11-11)** the design's worst-case total is 0.29% on ±5 V for a 10 K change since calibration (EN-1), 0.31% with the reference 20 K from its 25 °C value, and 0.39% with every change at 20 K (0.12 to 0.16% RSS). The reference's thermal hysteresis is not specified for that grade and is carried at the standard grade's 100 ppm *(check)*. With the standard grade, whose initial accuracy is ±0.2%, the total reaches 0.56% and fails without a meter calibration of every board |
| SC-8 | **Relaxed (2026-11-12, D19): gain within 5.5% before calibration, the 1% parts' worst case.** Channel matching, before host calibration: gain within 3% (2026-11-11; the range switch's resistance sits in the ±5 V range's gain), offset small enough that SC-2's centring margin holds at the ADC pin (2026-11-11: the offset itself is calibrated, SC-7), bandwidth within 10%. The gain is coarse because the host calibrates each channel on both ranges (PA-3); the bound still catches a mis-assembled part |
| SC-9 | Crosstalk: a full-scale input on one channel with the other input shorted changes the other channel's reading by at most 2 LSB at 1,022,727 sps (one channel) and 523,255 sps per channel (two channels), at the client's configured acquisition settings. The project records a 56% sample-and-hold memory on an undriven pin, so a low-impedance drive is expected The full-scale input may be DC, a step or a sine at any frequency up to 10 MHz (D13). With that ceiling the two scope channels share one dual op-amp, which the chosen TPH2502's datasheet supports at −100 dB channel to channel at 5 MHz (about −94 dB at 10 MHz by extrapolation *(check)*); **VT-10 on the first boards confirms it** (D16), and if it fails, each channel gets its own dual. The same 2 LSB limit applies to the AWG coupling into a scope channel while DAC0 plays (2026-11-11) |
| SC-10 | Source impedance at the ADC pin at most 5 kΩ, so the ADC settles to within 0.5 LSB inside its configured acquisition window and not the sample interval. The basis is the datasheet's 12-bit track time of 0.054 ns per ohm plus 205 ns *(check)* and 15 ADC clocks at 22.5 MHz (667 ns), which gives about 8.6 kΩ before margin |
| SC-11 | **Removed for every version (2026-11-12, D18): one range, ±5 V; no range switch.** The text below records the range as first decided. **Input ranges (decided 2026-11-10, D1).** Two ranges: the ±5 V range of SC-2 and SC-4, and **±1.25 V**, a level-shifting op-amp stage with an as-built gain of **1.10 to 1.16** into the ADC pin. At that gain ±1.25 V keeps at least 80 mV of margin per side inside 0 to AREF at AREF 3.20 V with ±30 mV of centring error, as SC-2 does for ±5 V; a ±1 V AWG signal fills 69 to 73% of ADC full scale, and the step is about 0.68 to 0.71 mV per code (derived). The stage is supplied from the AFE's own 3.3 V rail so it meets PR-2. A Due pin selects the range through a solid-state switch, placed after the input protection, and the ±5 V range is selected at reset and whenever the Due's pins are undriven. Both ranges keep SC-3's input resistance, PR-1's protection, SC-6's noise and SM-3's bandwidth. The Due's level band referred to the input is that band divided by the range's gain (section 5) |
| SC-12 | **Full-scale slew (2026-11-12, D16).** A full-scale sine at the input passes the scope stage undistorted (not slew-limited) up to at least 5 MHz, on every range. The design shows it from the chosen op-amp's slew rate at the stage's output swing, and VT-1's sine cases measure it |

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| DF-1 | Differential mode, with CH1 = IN+ and CH2 = IN−, is selected in host software and needs no jumper. The analog signal path is the same in both modes, so the mode is a host setting and not a hardware input |
| DF-2 | Two differential ranges, chosen by the firmware's PGA gain: ±5 V difference at most 4 mV per code, and ±10 V difference at most 7.5 mV per code. No trimmer is fitted (2026-11-11) |
| DF-4 | Accuracy after host calibration, for the mean of at least 1024 samples: on the ±5 V range ±0.5% of reading ±1 code; on the ±1.25 V range **±0.5% of reading ±9 codes** (2026-11-11; 1.5 times the design's worst case, from one model of the input stage's non-linearity, confirmed by VT-1), because the input stage's non-linearity and the converter's own differential non-linearity (up to about 1.4 codes) are not removed by a gain and offset fit. Bandwidth and noise per SM-3 and SC-6. On the ±1.25 V range the same two PGA gains give about ±1.25 V and ±2.5 V of difference; the contractor states the exact values |

## 8. AWG output (one channel, single-ended, from DAC0)


| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| AW-1 | Output ±1.0 V DC-coupled into a high-impedance load (±0.5 V into 50 Ω, AW-4). As built, including the AW-7 tolerances and AREF 3.20 to 3.30 V, reaching ±1.0 V uses at most 92% of the board's DAC window |
| AW-2 | Nominal step at most 1 mV: one DAC code is about 0.62 mV at the output. The DAC's own differential non-linearity (±3 LSB in the SAM3X datasheet *(check)*, so a worst single step of about 2.5 mV) is the Due's and is not an AFE limit; the host calibrates it if needed |
| AW-3 | **No reconstruction filter (decided 2026-11-10).** The DAC's output reaches the AWG output unfiltered, through a stage whose bandwidth is at least the scope path's (SM-3), so the 1.6 Msym/s test patterns are not shaped. **Its slew need only keep up with the DAC's own edge** (about 6 V/µs at the DAC pin *(check)* times the stage's gain, D13); the DAC cannot produce a full-scale 10 MHz sine, so the slew that would take is not a requirement. Images of the sample rate are not attenuated: a 100 kHz sine at 1 Msps has its first image about 19 dB down (derived, zero-order hold), and the host reduces them by oversampling, up to the DAC's 1.39 M updates/s. Gain error across the band is corrected by host calibration |
| AW-4 | **50 Ω back-terminated source (2026-11-10).** A 50 Ω ±1% series resistor at the output, after the output stage, drives any load from 50 Ω up: ±1 V into a high-impedance load such as a scope input, ±0.5 V into 50 Ω. The termination absorbs the reflection from an unterminated cable end, which matters on the wide path: a 1 m cable is about 5 ns against a 42 ns edge (section 4.2). A short to ground is safe indefinitely, and the resistor limits it to about 26 mA in the design's simulation, inside PW-3's 30 mA. Accuracy (AW-7) is stated into a high-impedance load; into 50 Ω the resistor's tolerance adds up to about 0.5% of amplitude, which host calibration may correct |
| AW-5 | Draws at most 0.1 mA from the DAC pin |
| AW-6 | AFE-added noise at most **0.3 mV rms (2026-11-12, D22)**, stated as a density over the output stage's whole bandwidth as SC-6 is. In a loopback the scope attenuates it (about 0.23 on version 1's ±5 V, about 0.46 on version 2's range), so 0.3 mV adds at most about 0.14 mV at the pin, and with the scope's 0.2 mV it widens the Due's 0.64 mV floor by about 7%, the same share the 0.1 mV figure was set for on the dropped ±1.25 V range (derived). Measured at the AWG output with DAC0 replaced by a quiet DC source at the same level. Total output noise with DAC0 driving is measured and reported, not guaranteed. The DAC's own noise is measured and reported before this figure is accepted. Verified as SC-6 is (AN-5, VT-3) |
| AW-7 | Accuracy: after calibration ±0.5% ±5 mV; the precision ADC reads the AWG output, so the host corrects gain and offset (2026-11-11). As built, gain and offset need only keep AW-1 (the ±1 V swing within 92% of the DAC window), so 1% parts may set them. **The AWG's offset reference is its own, from the AFE's 3.3 V rail (2026-11-11)**, fast enough to be present from power-up so AW-8 holds; it need not track AREF, because the offset is calibrated against the precision ADC |
| AW-8 | **(2026-11-11)** During Due reset or boot, with DAC0 anywhere from 0 V to 3.3 V as measured (section 4.1, about 3.3 V at the reset and about 0 V for seconds after boot), the output stays within ±2.1 V and returns to the commanded value once the firmware drives DAC0, without exceeding ±2.1 V on the way. The limit is set by what the AWG does with DAC0 at either rail (about −1.5 and +2.0 V in the design's simulation), so the documentation states it: anything connected to the AWG output may see up to ±2.1 V while the Due resets or boots |

## 9. Phase 3: a precision reference and a slow precision ADC

**Adopted (D5), on the same PCB as Phase 1.** Two parts. **3a** is a precision
voltage reference, a test point, and a routed connection to the
Due's AREF header pin. **3b** is a slow, precise ADC on that reference, which measures
the AWG output during a tee calibration (PA-3) and the ladder's taps, to calibrate the Due's own converters. They give absolute accuracy (gain, offset, and
the AREF value as the Due's ADC reads it) and, because the reference is a quiet DC
source, the ADC's own noise floor. The slow ADC reads DC values; noise above a
few hertz is measured by the Due's ADC on the reference (PA-8) or by an external
instrument (VT-3), not by 3b. INL (integral non-linearity: how far a converter's code against voltage curve strays from a straight line) alone does not justify them: the DAC and ADC together lie on a line to 0.3 to 0.5 codes over a 1.5 V window, small against the level spacings we use but not against a held-code noise of about 0.8 codes rms (section 4.1), and averaging removes noise and not INL. So INL is a reason for 3b only for averaged level work. 3a has no switch on the signal path, and neither does 3b:
its one switch is on the AWG-sense divider (PA-4).

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| LR-1 | **Two layers, one unbroken ground plane on the bottom, and no split (2026-11-12, D12).** The Due has one ground and the AFE has one: no separate analog and digital ground, no slot. The bottom layer carries no trace under the scope front ends, the AWG stage, the reference, the ladder or the precision ADC. The 10 mm is a design rule, not a simulation result |
| LR-6 | **Each rail's supply copper is a star.** The star point is the rail generator's output capacitors. The AWG and each scope channel that draws on a rail get their own branch, ending in their own filter at the load's end of the branch (in the first round, 1 Ω ahead of each scope op-amp's 10 µF and 22 Ω ∥ 22 Ω on the AWG's supply, as `sim/powercal` models them) and their local decoupling, so the AWG's load current never flows in copper a scope channel's supply also uses. **The star is for supply copper only**: every return goes through the one plane (LR-1), never a star ground. VT-10's AWG-into-scope crosstalk measures what is left |
| LR-7 | **The AWG's current loop is short and kept to its own zone.** DAC0's route from its header pin to the AWG buffer is short and over plane. The output resistor sits at the BNC. Each BNC shell is tied to the plane at the connector by several vias, or by its through-hole shell legs, which count, so a 50 Ω load's return current goes straight into the plane at the AWG's BNC and not across the scope zone. The AWG's output trace is electrically short at the stage's bandwidth, so it is not routed as a controlled impedance; its length is stated |
| LR-8 | **The reference is routed by Kelvin.** The reference's output has separate traces from its pin to the ladder's top, to each precision-ADC input or reference pin it feeds, and to the AREF header pin through the unfitted 0 Ω (PA-7), so ladder current flows in no trace the precision ADC reads through. The ladder's resistors sit together, and so do the sense divider's, each in one orientation and away from the op-amps' heat, so their parts drift together. Whatever measures the sense divider's ratio (PA-4) sits at the precision ADC's pins |
| LR-9 | **Digital lines stay at the edge.** The precision ADC's bus and every other Due digital line the AFE uses run along the board's edges, by the edge headers or along a bare edge, outside every analog zone and LR-5's keep-out, and never cross an analog zone on either layer (D14). **A static control line** (a range or ratio switch select, which changes only when the setting does) **may enter its own block's zone** by the shortest path from the nearest edge pin, with an RC at the switch; it never crosses another channel, a divider's high-impedance side or an ADC trace. Clock and data lines keep the strict rule |
| LR-10 | **The board house's rules, with margin.** 0.15 mm trace and space and a 0.3 mm via hole at least, so no extra charge (`hardware/afe-proto/docs/jlcpcb.md`), with KiCad 10.x's design rule check set to those values, and clean |
| LR-11 | **The board reads without the schematic (owner, 2026-11-12).** Parts are placed in functional blocks, one contiguous area each, so that a person with only the board can find and fix a fault: **CH1, CH2, AWG, POWER, REF and CAL** (the reference, the ladder, the precision ADC and the calibration header), and the I²C and digital edge. Each block is outlined on the silkscreen and named in it, signal flow runs one way through it (LR-2) with an arrow where it is not obvious, and the BNCs, the CAL header and its "on" position are labelled. **Reference designators are numbered by block** (for example 1xx for CH1, 2xx for CH2, 3xx for the AWG, 4xx for power, 5xx for the reference and calibration), so a designator says where its part is. Every designator is printed beside its part, legible at the board house's minimum silkscreen size. **Each block has a labelled test point on its key node** (each channel's ADC pin, the AWG output before its back-termination, each rail, the reference), on top, reachable with the shield on the Due. Where the board is tight, it spreads out rather than drop a label (LR-1, D12) |

## 12. Verification


All tests use calibrated sources and instruments whose uncertainty is at
most one third of the tolerance tested. For DC tests that is about ±0.17%
±1.7 mV (5½-digit meter class), and DF-4 needs ±1.1 mV. The noise tests
need an instrument whose own floor is at most a third of the limit: about
0.07 mV rms over the wide path's bandwidth for SC-6, 0.03 mV for AW-6 and
0.1 mV over DC to 1 MHz for PW-4 (VT-3, VT-8, VT-16). The Due's ADC cannot
provide that, and the owner's 8-bit scope cannot either; where no such
instrument is borrowed, SC-6 and AW-6 rest on simulation (AN-5) and on their
measured effect in VT-15. The client lends
bench instruments, and the test procedure lists them.
