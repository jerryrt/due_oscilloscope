a regenerated baseline is not comparable to this one without saying

**The amplitude difference is this bench's board, and it is inside
tolerance.** The Track B report first called it "about six codes above
the theoretical maximum"; that framing is withdrawn there and here.
1370.5 is not a ceiling - it is 2047 digital DAC codes times
`calibration.json`'s `loop_slope_adc_per_dac_code` of 0.67053, a gain
measured on **one** board and read from a committed file by every bench.
This bench implies 1376.65 / 2047 = 0.6725, **+0.30%**, against a span
tolerance that file states as +/-1.8%.

What the second track adds is still worth having: Track A reads
**1376.69 against Track B's 1376.61**, two independent register
programmings agreeing to 0.08 codes, so whatever the 0.30% is, it is not
firmware - which is what the oracle is for. Board or wiring, and nothing
here separates those.

**Overruns are track-dependent and the two hosts agree about it.** Track
A 32 (windows) against 35 (linux); Track B 55 against 63. Same ordering,
same rough magnitude, on two hosts and two boards. macOS is the outlier
on both tracks at 6-8, on n=3 against n=9. So "Linux overruns more" from
the Track B report was the wrong reading: **Track B overruns more than
Track A, on every host that has measured both**, and macOS is the one
that needs explaining.

**The RC 44 / RC 39 ladder deficit reproduces across tracks too** -
0.165% and 0.147% here against Track B's 0.176% and 0.147%, with 0 B at
the other five rates on both. Two independent programmings landing on
the same two rates is consistent with the documented slow converter
being the device's, not either track's. It still disagrees with this
bench's own `tools/writepolicy.py` runs, which read 0 B in 16 of 16 at
those two rates with a different feeder; that disagreement is recorded
on the Track B baseline and is not resolved.

**Provenance note.** Unlike the Track B report this one carries no
"predates a firmware commit" flag, because the Track A image genuinely
was rebuilt after the newest `sketches/` commit. The cross-track false
positive described on the Track B baseline is therefore visible by
contrast here, which is the clearest evidence that `FW_SOURCE` wants
splitting per track.
